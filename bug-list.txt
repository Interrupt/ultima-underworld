It is NOT the actual blocker, though -- live instrumentation (added DEBUG(TRACE) calls in play_music_track/set_music_enabled, still present in src/audio.c) found the real chain, in order:
  1. DAT_00087454/DAT_00087448 (src/audio.c): the "audio subsystem initialized"/"music enabled" gate flags are permanently 0 -- no writer exists anywhere in this decompile (grepped exhaustively) -- so play_music_track, resume_music_playback and set_music_enabled take their very first early-out branch on literally every call, every run.   2. cpp_operator_new (src/ordinal_stubs.c) is ALSO a hardcoded NULL-returning stub -- the second half of the same gap. Together, these two bugs mean construct_and_load_mod_player has apparently never executed even once since this was decompiled.
  3. Fixing both (tried live) immediately exposes a THIRD, previously-invisible bug: construct_and_load_mod_player SIGSEGVs on its first real run. Root cause: every MOD-engine "dynamic array" struct (pattern/instrument/channel-state/etc., ~20 functions across ~1400 lines of audio.c) stores its allocation in a 4-byte (undefined4) field and reads it back later as a real address -- faithful to the original 32-bit ARM binary's pointer width, but on this native 64-bit recompile no allocation can land below 4GB (confirmed empirically, not just in theory: calloc() and even mmap(MAP_FIXED) at every low address tried from 0x1000000 to 0xff000000 are refused by the kernel on arm64 macOS), so the stored pointer silently truncates and the next read-back dereferences a wrong, almost always unmapped address.
Fixing #3 for real needs either an offset-shifting struct refactor across the whole MOD engine or a guaranteed-low-address allocator (confirmed infeasible on this platform) -- bigger than one pass. Shipping fixes for #1/#2 alone would trade silence for a guaranteed crash the moment anyone enables music (every game startup calls play_music_track(1,1) for the splash-screen tune), so those were reverted back to original (silent, stable) behavior rather than shipped half-done; see src/audio.c's DAT_00087454 comment and src/ordinal_stubs.c's cpp_operator_new comment for the full writeup. waveOutOpen/waveOutWrite were NOT touched since reaching them is still blocked upstream -- implementing a real SDL2 backend behind them would be unverifiable scaffolding until #1-#3 are resolved. Next step for whoever picks this up: resolve #3 first (it's the load-bearing blocker), then #1/#2 are a one-line-each flip, then waveOut* becomes the easy, well-scoped remainder.
